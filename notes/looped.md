# Looped k3 — separate experiment track

Opened 2 Sep 2026 as a deliberate split: the pretraining line (training loop, optimizer, data
pipeline) keeps its own schedule and is **not** blocked by anything here. This track builds
model variants and compares them; choosing which config runs, and when, is a separate
decision from writing the code.

Config table is regenerated by `uv run python -m scripts.loop_budget`. Dated measurements go
to `notes/ledger.md`, not here.

---

## 1. The question

At equal budget, does looping a span of layers — running the same weights a second time on
their own output — beat an unlooped stack of the same cost, at 200–350M total params on one
6 GB card?

**"Equal budget" is the entire experiment.** A loop adds FLOPs. Comparing a looped model to
an unlooped one of the same *size* measures the extra FLOPs and calls it an architecture
win — that is the methodological error SMELT was written to correct. Every looped config
below is matched to an unlooped control on three budgets at once: per-token FLOPs, total
parameters, KV-cache slots.

Secondary question, cheaper and possibly more informative at this scale: **does the second
visit reduce the attention sink?** SMELT's mechanistic claim (§6.4) is that on the second
visit the mass a head parks on the BOS token collapses and moves onto content tokens. We
have 3–4 softmax MLA layers; the measurement is a few lines on any checkpoint and does not
need scale to be real.

## 2. Source: SMELT, arXiv 2609.01343 (1 Sep 2026)

"Sparse MoE transformer, **M**iddle layers **L**oop **T**wice". Recipe (Figure 4a, verified
in the paper HTML, not taken from a summary):

1. loop the contiguous **middle 50% of layers, exactly twice** (L=12 → layers 4–9, 18
   executions);
2. **narrow the hidden dim** to give the FLOPs back (200M: H 1280 → 1056);
3. **raise the expert count** to give the parameters back (192 → 288 per layer);
4. **shrink head size, raise the GQA ratio** to give the KV cache back;
5. **scale the residual update of sublayers inside the loop by 1/r = 1/2** — otherwise the
   same weights write into the residual stream twice in a correlated direction and inflate
   it.

Their worked example lands at +2.9% FLOPs, +0.4% params, KV within 4% of the control. That
is the tolerance band this track copies.

Results: 6.8–18.0% of training FLOPs saved on the compute-optimal frontier, fitted over
1.3e19–2.2e21 FLOPs, up to 54B non-embedding params. Gain grows with compute, is larger at
*lower* sparsity (10.0% at S≈85% vs 6.8% at S≈97%, C=1e20), largest on Code (20.4% at
C=1e21), grows with sample length (1.52× short→long) and with the number of in-context
examples.

Mechanism: on the second visit a head's attention mass on BOS drops 0.60 → 0.02 and mass on
demonstration answer tokens rises 0.24 → 0.85. Across the whole model, an unlooped stack's
sink mass *grows* with depth, while the second visit sits *below* the first throughout the
looped span. The two visits are not redundant and not opposed: cosine between the residual
writes of the same physical layer on the two visits is 0.42–0.65 (0.16 for non-matching
pairs) — visit 2 amplifies what visit 1 established.

Loop count is fixed at r=2. **No test-time claim**: no early exit, no adaptive depth, no
latent-reasoning story. Adaptive depth and per-visit LoRA are listed as future work. Do not
conflate this with Huginn (2502.05171) or Ouro (2510.25741), where the loop count varies at
inference and *that* is the point.

Sparsity, in their definition (Eq. 1: S = 1 − N_act_eq/N against a fully active control):
Config F is 1 − 45.6/246.1 ≈ **81.5%**, just under their lowest sparse cell (85%). We are in
their grid, at the end where the gain was largest.

## 3. What does not transfer to k3 verbatim

Three places where our stack is not their baseline and the recipe needs a decision of its
own. Each is an ablation, not a settled answer.

**(a) We have no ordinary residual, so step 5 has no obvious home.** SMELT scales `f` in
`x ← x + f(x)`. We have Block AttnRes: `partial = partial + out` inside a block, read later
by a softmax over sources. The failure mode is the same (one set of weights writing twice
into one sum, in correlated directions), so the translation is to scale the contribution of
looped sublayers:

```python
partial = out if partial is None else partial + scale * out   # scale = 1/r inside the span
```

But the softmax over sources partly normalizes scale by itself, which their architecture
does not do. **Run it both ways.**

**(b) `AttnRes.q` — one query per sublayer, or one per visit?** The second visit sees a
strictly larger source list (`blocks + [partial]`, where `partial` already contains visit-1
output). Sharing one learned query across two different source sets is odd. A per-visit
query costs `d_model` = 512 floats per sublayer. Their equivalent (per-visit LoRA) is
future work; ours is nearly free.

**(c) QB and the router.** `LatentMoE._scores.append` fires once per visit, so a looped
layer contributes 2m rows to the quantile and both visits share one `qb_bias`. Visit
statistics differ — they measured V-vectors diverging across visits (cosine 0.65–0.74)
much more than Q/K (0.89–0.93) — so a per-visit `qb_bias` (one buffer of size `n_routed`)
is arguably the correct default. Cheap to test both.

**Placement constraint we do not get to choose.** `is_mla = (layer + 1) % 4 == 0`, so the
backbone ends with global attention **only when `n_layers % 4 == 0`**. L=18 and L=10 end on
a KDA layer and break the K3 rule; every config below is a multiple of 4. This is also why
the looped span is picked to make KV slots match exactly rather than following SMELT's
⌊(L−m)/2⌋ placement blindly — see the table.

**Inference, when we get there.** The second visit recomputes Q/K/V from the updated
residual and does not reuse the first visit's cache (SMELT §6.3). So a looped MLA layer
needs *two* KV caches and a looped KDA layer needs *two* recurrent states S. That is what
the KV column below counts.

## 4. The five configs

Two execution budgets (16 and 24 layer-executions), one unlooped control each. `exec M` =
parameters actually executed per token, including embedding — the FLOPs proxy. `KV` = number
of MLA executions, i.e. cache slots at inference. All matched within the SMELT tolerance.

| # | config | L | loop span (0-idx) | r | exec | routed | total | Δ | exec M | Δ | KV |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | **A16** control (= Config F) | 16 | — | — | 16 | 64 | 225.7M | — | 48.7 | — | 4 |
| 2 | **L12/4×2** | 12 | 4–7 | 2 | 16 | 90 | 224.7M | −0.43% | 48.9 | +0.41% | 4 |
| 3 | **A24** control | 24 | — | — | 24 | 64 | 340.4M | — | 69.0 | — | 6 |
| 4 | **L16/8×2** | 16 | 4–11 | 2 | 24 | 103 | 341.0M | +0.19% | 69.5 | +0.67% | 6 |
| 5 | **L12/6×3** | 12 | 4–9 | 3 | 24 | 143 | 339.6M | −0.21% | 70.3 | +1.86% | **5** |

Everything else stays at Config F: `d_model=512`, 2 heads × 128, `moe_latent=256`,
`expert_hidden=256`, `top_k=4`, `n_shared=2`, `n_blocks=4` (over **executions**, not
layers).

How the numbers come out:

- **Params are restored with experts, not width.** SMELT had to narrow H because their
  control had no spare lever; we do — one routed expert costs `3 · ℓ · expert_hidden` =
  196 608 params per MoE layer, so at L=12 each extra expert is 2.16M and at L=16 it is
  2.95M. That is why `routed` climbs from 64 to 90/103/143 and `d_model` never moves. Keeping
  the width fixed also keeps per-execution FLOPs identical by construction, which is why the
  `exec M` column matches to well under 2%.
- **Span placement is chosen for KV, not by their formula.** For #2 the middle-4 span 4–7
  contains exactly one MLA layer (index 7), so 3 distinct + 1 repeat = 4 slots = A16. For #4
  the middle-8 span 4–11 contains MLA 7 and 11 → 4 + 2 = 6 = A24. SMELT's rule ⌊(L−m)/2⌋
  would put #2 at 4–7 anyway and #4 at 4–11 anyway; the constraint only bites at other L.
- **#5 is the one budget that does not match: 5 KV slots against A24's 6 (−17%), in the
  looped model's favour.** Report it; do not quietly call #5 matched. Its purpose is a
  three-way at fixed 24 executions — same effective depth reached by 24 distinct layers
  (#3), by 16 layers with 8 doubled (#4), or by 12 layers with 6 tripled (#5). SMELT's own
  sweep (Table 3.5) put r=2 ahead of r=3 and r=4 at their scale; #5 tests whether that holds
  when parameters are scarcer.
- **#2 is off the optimal span.** 4 of 12 is 33%, and their sweep peaked at 50%. It is here
  because it is the only 16-execution loop that satisfies the `n_layers % 4 == 0` constraint,
  and 16 executions is the budget that actually fits the card today.

## 5. Memory reality on this card, and what it implies

Measured 2 Sep 2026, details and harness in `notes/ledger.md`. nvidia-smi reports 6144 MiB
total with the Windows desktop already holding several hundred; usable is roughly 5.2–5.5
GiB before the allocator starts spilling to host memory.

The short version:

- **A16 at T=1536 fits and nothing else does.** 4354 MiB for forward+backward with grads,
  445 tok/s.
- **Adding fp32 AdamW state to A16 does not fit** — 6151 MiB, and throughput collapses to
  168 tok/s because the allocator pages to host. This is a fact about the *training track*,
  not about looping: at 225.7M params, fp32 weights + grads + two moments is 3.6 GiB before
  a single activation. The training loop will need bf16 or 8-bit optimizer state, or
  gradient checkpointing, or both.
- **A24 at T=1536 does not fit** — 6235 MiB, 116 tok/s, spilling. At T=1024 it fits (4770
  MiB, 343 tok/s).

Consequence for this track: **budget pair 1 (#1 vs #2) is runnable today at T=1536; budget
pair 2 (#3, #4, #5) needs T ≤ 1024 or gradient checkpointing.** Run pair 1 first.

Two structural notes on looped memory, to be checked once the variants exist rather than
assumed:

- Activation memory scales with **executions**, weight memory with **distinct layers**. #2
  should therefore land slightly *below* A16 (same 16 executions, 4 fewer layers of weights;
  L=12 alone at T=1536 measures 3257 MiB against A16's 4354). Looping is memory-cheaper than
  depth at equal compute — which on this card may matter more than any loss difference.
- ~~Gradient checkpointing collides with `LatentMoE.forward` mutating `self._scores`.~~
  **Fixed 2 Sep:** `forward` writes `_visit_scores[visit]` (a dict keyed by visit number,
  so a recompute overwrites rather than appends) and accumulation happens in
  `harvest_scores()`, called from the training loop after backward. Gradient checkpointing
  itself is still not implemented.

## 6. Implementation — **built 2 Sep**

`K3Config` has `loop_span`, `loop_r`, `loop_res_scale`; `K3Model.body` walks the execution
order; AttnRes blocks are cut over executions; layer type stays keyed to the layer index;
`count_params` returns a third number, `executed`. The five configs are named in
`model/configs.py` and the budget table below is reproduced by
`uv run python -m scripts.loop_budget` and asserted in `tests/test_loop.py`.

Measured on the real modules, T=1536, full training step: **A16 1146 tok/s / 5098 MiB vs
L12_4x2 1293 tok/s / 5059 MiB** — the looped variant is both faster and slightly cheaper in
memory at matched budget, as §5 predicted. **No training comparison has been run.**

The sketch that guided it, kept because it explains the shape of the code:

- `K3Config` gains `loop_span: tuple[int, int] | None` and `loop_r: int = 1`.
- `forward` iterates over an execution order, not `range(n_layers)`:
  `list(range(a)) + list(range(a, b+1)) * r + list(range(b+1, L))` (this is
  `scripts/loop_budget.execution_order`, already written and used for the table).
- Block boundaries must be cut over **executions**: `per_block = len(order) // n_blocks`,
  and the `n_layers % n_blocks == 0` assertion in `__post_init__` becomes an assertion on
  `len(order)`.
- Layer *type* stays keyed to the layer index (`(layer + 1) % attn_pattern`), never to the
  position in the execution order. Today those coincide; with a loop they do not.
- `count_params()` needs a third number. `active` currently counts distinct parameters and
  will understate a looped model; the honest quantity is per-execution active summed over
  the execution order — the same arithmetic `scripts/loop_budget.py` already does.

## 7. Protocol

1. Implement the loop and confirm the budget table on the real modules: `loop_budget`
   numbers reproduce, one forward runs, shapes hold, and every parameter still receives a
   gradient (the `grok_review/probes.py` check — with weight sharing, a looped layer's
   gradient must be the sum over its visits).
2. **Pair 1 only**, short: #1 vs #2, ~300–500M tokens, **two seeds each**. Four runs. Log
   val loss, tok/s, peak memory.
3. Read the result honestly. A difference smaller than the seed-to-seed spread is not a
   result. If pair 1 shows nothing, that is the answer at this scale and the track closes —
   record it and stop; do not escalate to pair 2 hoping for a bigger effect.
4. If pair 1 shows something, or shows nothing but the attention-sink measurement (below)
   does: pair 2 at T=1024, plus the (a)/(b)/(c) ablations from §3, one at a time.
5. Attention sink, on any checkpoint from step 2, regardless of the loss outcome: mean
   attention mass on token 0 per MLA head, visit 1 vs visit 2. This is the cheapest
   reproduction of a real result in the paper and it does not need the loss to move.

## 8. What to expect

Compute: 6 · 45.6e6 · 4e9 tokens ≈ **1.2e18 FLOPs** for a full pretrain here, an order of
magnitude *below* the bottom of SMELT's fitted window (1.3e19). Their gain grows with
compute, and extrapolating a scaling fit below its window is not valid. So the realistic
expectation is the low end of their range or nothing measurable above seed noise — and a
short 300–500M-token pair sits further below that still.

That is not an argument against running it. It is an argument for running the cheap version
first, with two seeds, and for treating the attention-sink measurement as the part most
likely to produce a real observation at this scale.
